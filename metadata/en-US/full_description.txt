Cairn is an offline-first trail map and route planner for Washington hikers. It shows what a
general hiking app will not: active fire perimeters, smoke and air quality, closures, water
sources, and permit boundaries, drawn on top of USGS topo and OpenStreetMap trails. It works
with airplane mode on. No account. No subscription for the core app. No ads, no analytics, no
tracking.

Features:

* Browse trails on USGS Topo, satellite, or an outdoors vector basemap, all with hillshade
  and contour lines traced on the phone, labeled in your units.
* Forest roads from the Forest Service motor vehicle use map: tap one for what may drive it
  and when, its surface, and its maintenance level.
* Plan a route: tap waypoints, snap to trails, get distance, elevation gain, and a
  grade-colored elevation profile.
* Record a hike with the screen off: distance, moving time, pace, gain, and pack-aware
  calories.
* Conditions: NIFC fire perimeters, air quality, NWS weather at the trailhead and the high
  point, red flag warnings, daylight and moon.
* Download regions for fully offline use: tiles, terrain, trails, forest roads, land
  boundaries, and the overlays you choose.
* Import and export GPX.
* Optional end-to-end encrypted multi-device sync. Off by default; nothing leaves your
  device readable.

Your location and your hikes stay on your device. Map requests send only a coordinate or a
map area to public data sources.

Data from OpenStreetMap contributors, USGS, the USDA Forest Service, Mapzen / AWS Terrain
Tiles, NIFC, NOAA, and Open-Meteo.
